TITLE: When the Multiple Eats the Engine
DATE: 2026-06-01

Inspired by a Substack comment: someone’s held this bank 6 years and says it’s done nothing. Fair, so let’s run the actual math on why, and then the part that matters more, the asymmetry of the next 5 years and how to set up for it.
Well, you need to understand the credit cycles if you are investing in banking and financial sectors, and we are currently at the top of the credit cycle, which is about to turn.
Secondly, I don’t know what your return expectations are from HDFC Bank, but if you are looking for anything more than 10-12% from them at this scale, then you should reallocate, not at the current valuation, but whenever the next structural rebound comes.
It was insanely priced for the size it was trading at, and the real value of HDFC Bank and the franchise was created by the founding team, which is gone.
Still, it’s a window of opportunity for anyone going fresh, because banks of this scale and network usually emerge stronger, and the multiple re-rates to around 18-20 in the future. It happened with ICICI after the Chanda Kochhar incident, and it will happen with HDFC Bank, but slower, and for a different reason.
ICICI re-rated because its ROE went from 7% to 17-18% because of an asset-quality cleanup, and the broken book was fixed, which led to a spring uncoiling effect over the last few years. HDFC has no such spring, because its book is already clean, so there’s no NPA recovery to re-rate against. The re-rate has to come from the slower, mechanical repair of margins instead.
And one can position for the front-loading of returns, because there are 2-3 catalysts over the long run.
Their NIM was around 4.1 pre-merger, but now it’s around the 3.38 range, because it inherited HDFC Ltd’s high-cost borrowings and lower-yielding mortgage book, and that’s dragging spreads down. So the real fuel here is those high-cost borrowings slowly moving off the book and the synergy coming into the picture.
The rate-cut cycle is already happening now, and in the near term it’s a headwind for banks, not a tailwind, because loans reprice down faster than deposits, and deposit repricing only becomes fuel on a lag, once the cuts stop.
So as the borrowings roll off and deposits eventually reprice lower, that becomes the fuel for margin and ROE expansion, which can ultimately lead to a re-rating. But that’s more of a FY27-28 timeline rather than an immediate catalyst.
At the same time, HDFC is a large part of the index, and post-merger the FII headroom has reopened, so its index weight can pull in passive and active reallocation flows over time. So there are multiple levers that can contribute in that direction.
But when people paid roughly 28-30 multiples, that was insanely priced for that size and growth rate, because the market is adjusting for the credit cycle plus the challenges they are facing after the merger. And now the compression has eaten away everything the EPS engine made.
So EPS moved from 17 to 49, which is 188% growth. But it’s a blended number that hides a regime change.
Almost all of it was the pre-merger franchise compounding at 18-20%; post-merger, the per-share growth has been 7-10% only. So the engine you’re actually buying forward runs at high single digits. And if you want to understand why, look at the loan growth itself against the system.
FY24 excluded; both HDFC’s jump and the system figure are distorted by the merger reclassification. FY25 onward is clean like-for-like.
So here’s how I’d frame the road ahead. Because India is a consumption-driven economy with a lot of system credit growth still to come, and given the economic size and growth rates we’re looking at, I’ll take a base of just 10% for the system, and HDFC Bank has every ability to deliver more than that, though realistically system plus 2-4 points, not the old double-digit premium. One more reason: India’s credit-to-GDP sits at roughly 93%, well below the credit penetration of developed economies, so there’s a long runway. So 10-12% is a reasonable base. And because HDFC, post-merger, has a more secured layer of lending, it’s aligned with the safer, slower part of the consumption story rather than chasing unsecured personal loans.
But it also tells you something important: the gap won’t return to 10 points, that was the law of large numbers, but a system-plus grower at this safety profile is still a reasonable base to underwrite.
And the compression of multiples has eaten into your return profile. This is the part that matters most, so look at it directly.
EPS bonus-adjusted; P/E trailing, approximate annual . Part of the de-rating is justified by ROE falling from 18% to 14%, the gap from a fair 18-20x down to today’s 15x is the dislocation.
If it’s a 7-8-year hold (that EPS of 17 is FY18, so if you bought near the peak multiple that clock is closer to 7-8 years, which only sharpens the point), you must have paid roughly 510 going in, basically 17 EPS multiplied by 30 PE. And today it’s 49 EPS multiplied by 15 PE, which lands at 735.
From here, given the size and scale, and a reversion toward 20 in the future, though that’s not automatic; it needs ROE back to 16-17%, and today it sits at 14%.
But going forward, even if I give it a 12% EPS growth assumption, over the next 5 years you land at an EPS close to 86, and a multiple of 20 gives the stock something around 1,724. Now you see the setup for an 18-19% CAGR, because you have both engines in favour.
Even if I give a 10% growth rate on EPS at a 20 multiple, it’s a 16.5% CAGR over the next 5 years.
That’s how it works, mathematically and rationally.
Now you have to understand the credit cycle part as well. HDFC will be better positioned when it happens, because its book is now mortgage-heavy and secured, and the stress this cycle is in unsecured retail and MFI, which they barely touch.
And because it’s insulated from the segment that’s cracking, the credit turn won’t hand you a panic discount the way an unsecured-heavy lender would.
So for this bank specifically, you should focus on the margin turn, where their NIM is bottoming, the CD ratio normalizing, and the rate-cut cycle ending. That cycle is when you have to add more if you want the asymmetry of the next cycle, or simply prefer inactivity if you are already holding.
And to be clear, banking isn’t this simple.
