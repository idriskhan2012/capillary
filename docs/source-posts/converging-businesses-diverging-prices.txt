TITLE: Converging Businesses, Diverging Prices
DATE: 2026-05-20

Either Titan is insanely overvalued, or Kalyan is deeply undervalued. Both can’t be right.
Here’s the math behind why I think the market is mispricing one of them, and why my fresh capital is going to Kalyan for the next decade.
Two reasons I’m choosing Kalyan over adding to Titan from here.
One, I am holding Titan since 2017 in my family account. So this is already a settled position.
Secondly, from here I need to calculate the fresh return profile and the net gain on my capital because of opportunity cost. And when I run that math, Kalyan has a better probability to deliver over the next 10 years in comparison to Titan.
The reasoning being that Titan doesn’t have the multiple engine in its favor anymore, and even the growth engine rates are moderating relative to the valuation. In Kalyan, the growth rates are better than Titan, plus the multiples I’m getting are much better. So I have both the engines.
Titan is around 70 PE, so even if EPS moves up by 20% every year, the PE compression can dilute the return profile. But Kalyan is now around 20-24 PE, and a lot of things are converging in Kalyan’s favor. The pledges are strategically being reduced, which will further reduce the interest cost. Plus, they have gone for the FOCO model, which is leading to a very massive growth rate in store count, and the same-store sales growth is also increasing.
And one more thing. Even the South fortress where Kalyan has been dominating, they are shifting their COCO model to FOCO in that ecosystem as well. They are trading short-term structural pain for long-term shareholder value.
I will use elements of the linguistic mental model to decode it for you. So on the South and FOCO transition. In the Q2 FY26 concall, BOB Capital’s analyst pressed Ramesh on why COCO store revenue had degrown by 1% despite the gold price tailwind, citing the steady deceleration from 17-18% growth all the way down to negative territory. Ramesh’s response named it directly. This is the South COCO to FOCO conversion playing out.
So you can see the South COCO degrowth that looks like weakness on the surface for the analyst is actually a deliberate capital efficiency strategy, because it will lead to further ROCE expansion. So they are structurally changing the business while the market reacts to noise.
That’s one signal of allocator discipline. Here’s another. Now see how they have addressed the debt and pledge question and decode the pattern across four consecutive concalls.
So now you can see the progression and direction of how the pledging is going to be reduced. And one more thing, a smart allocator always under-promises and over-delivers. Caplin does that. So does Intuit in the US. The promoters who keep over-promising usually end up destroying shareholder value, and the Indian corporate structure from top to bottom is filled with that. So this is also a small pattern you can keep in mind when you track management calls. Most jewellery retailers are leverage-trapped, but Kalyan is methodically de-leveraging its structure.
Now Kalyan is the only meaningful challenger to Titan, which obviously I had critical views on when it was trading at 100-120 multiples. But now, because the odds have shifted, mathematically the next 10 years return profile of Kalyan has a very high probability of being better. The growth rates are high, but even if I give them the same growth rates as Titan, the multiple expansion will still be there in Kalyan because the narrative around it will slowly start fading away.
Plus, I’m decoding the linguistic language of the promoters as well, so the direction is strategically aligning with the mathematical odds. Earlier, they had both the operational metrics plus the storytelling metrics, but because of all the noise which the analysts and media have created, they have just gone fully toward operational metrics. You will see in the last 1 year of financial reports that it starts with crude numbers rather than the story. And you can see the same de-storytelling pattern in his concall openings, across four consecutive quarters.
So you can see the mindset of an allocator says, okay, if you’re not listening to whether we are transparent, we’ll show you through the numbers themselves.
The clearest moment came in the Q3 FY26 concall. A retail investor named Srinivas from Pheonix Estate, frustrated with the stock’s underperformance, pressed Ramesh directly on the disconnect between the strong results and the falling stock price. Ramesh refused to engage with the share price question, twice.
That phrase, “not under our control”, is unusual in Indian corporate concall culture, because promoters typically spend half their time managing share price expectations and will bring all the macro and micro excuses to hedge it.
Kalyan’s promoters delivered it without apology, without hedge, without macro excuse. And they learned it the hard way last year, because they were showing transparency by calling immediate press conferences, and the whole media created havoc and conspiracy theories and dragged it further. The market is doing what the market does. The business is doing what the business does. And the management is calmer now.
This is also a signal of the entire thesis in one exchange. The analyst and retail investor are seeing mispricing and panicking, while the management sees mispricing and keeps executing. The people who exploit that gap are the ones who create real alpha in the markets.
And there’s one more piece of language that aligns with the operator profile. In the Q3 concall, Ramesh repeated the phrase 
“it cannot be done overnight”
 three times, on inventory adjustment after gold price spikes, on 18-karat acceptance in different states, and on the franchisee margin renegotiation taking time to show up in numbers. Three different topics, but same temporal honesty. This is operator language, and always screen for operator language vs marketing language in any transcript. He keeps reminding the market and these analysts that retail jewellery is a slow-turn business and the right things take time to show up.
The language tells one story. The numbers tell another, equally strong.
Now coming to the second part. The expansion plans and the marketing style of Kalyan are much better than Titan right now. And they are also going for regional brands where, in every state, they will have regional brands as well, which will maximize the shift from unorganized to organized.
And there’s one more structural margin lever that the market is not yet pricing. About 12 months ago, Kalyan renegotiated its franchisee terms, capex sharing, expense sharing, and margin sharing, to capture an additional 25-50 bps of gross margin per franchise store. This is now showing up in the numbers as new and renewed franchise stores roll onto the new terms. It is pure pricing power being exercised over a previously-weaker franchisee position, because franchise demand has exceeded supply, and the brand is becoming strong enough that franchisees now benefit more from Kalyan than Kalyan benefits from any individual franchisee.
The valuation math.
If I compare the revenue difference between Kalyan and Titan, Titan has close to 87,000 crores in revenue while Kalyan has around 35,000 crores. When it comes to profitability, Titan has something around 5,000 crores and Kalyan has something around 1,350 crores.
And then the operating profit gap, which is the more honest number to look at. In early 2025, the operating profit gap between Kalyan and Titan was around 2.59x. Now it has reduced to 2.35x. I’m taking the operating difference because the interest cost is currently diluting Kalyan’s EPS, and that will further improve as the pledges come down. So the real difference between both the models’ operational performance is around 2.5x.
So to put it together. The revenue gap is around 1.5x, the operating profit gap is around 2.5x, the net profit gap is roughly 3-4x (inflated by interest cost), but the market cap gap is close to 10x. That is a huge anomaly and arbitrage in terms of valuation gaps. The business structures are converging, but the market cap is diverging.
The business structures are converging, but the market cap is diverging.
And now you can see mathematically where the excess liquidity lies. Whether it sits in Titan or in Kalyan, you have to ask the question. Even if I give Titan the margin premium it deserves, it still doesn’t justify a 10x difference in market cap. The premium argument breaks down at this gap.
Plus, Kalyan still has room for margin expansion as they move forward and economies of scale start kicking in, so that itself is another engine of growth.
So the mathematical odds have just gotten better. If you go through the two-engine framework, you will understand the why. And then Titan has the headwind of the law of large numbers, and Kalyan doesn’t. As Titan moves towards a 5 or 6 lakh crore market cap, the size itself will lead to restraints on market cap expansion, and the multiples can compress. But Kalyan has a massive growth runway because it is still a very small market cap right now.
This is what I call a layered margin of safety. It’s not one thing going right. It’s multiple things going right at the same time, while the market is pricing for things going wrong.
The historical mirror.
The same arbitrage which I’m calling right now existed in reverse earlier.
When Kalyan was trading at those high multiples, the market cap had reached close to 1 lakh crore. And Titan’s market cap at that time was close to where it is trading right now, around 3.5 lakh crore. So at that time, the market cap difference was only around 4x, and the net profit difference was also around 4x. So then you could see the mathematical symmetry. The market cap difference was close to the profitability difference and aligned with the business fundamentals.
Today, the asymmetry is the opposite. The market cap difference is close to 10x, while the revenue difference is almost the same and continuously converging, it is now less than 1.5x, and the operational profitability difference is also converging and is around 2.5x. So the underlying business structure has improved and is coming closer to Titan, but the market cap arbitrage has widened massively.
Over a long enough period, that asymmetry has to correct. Either the business catches down to the market cap, or the market cap catches up to the business. And given everything converging in Kalyan’s favor, my bet is on the second.
This is how you feel the business to its essence.
 When the mathematical, the linguistic language, the capital allocation strategy, the engine math, the growth math, and the margin of safety math, everything starts aligning in one direction.
