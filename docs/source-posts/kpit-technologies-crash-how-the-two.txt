TITLE: KPIT Technologies Crash: How the Two-Engine Trap Creates a Lost Decade
DATE: 2026-07-01

KPIT dragged 16% today to 563, hit the lower circuit. And from 1,865 to 563 now, just to recover and break even, you need a 3x outcome. Sit with that.
And this is not some random crash. This is a Q1 warning, European auto is cutting R&D, BMW and VW are bleeding, and the whole ER&D pack is getting repriced.
Here is the thing you have to understand. When you buy at a peak multiple and a peak earning, then 
both the engines start working against you, and that is catastrophic.
And that is exactly what is happening with KPIT. The multiple engine is compressing, and at the same time the earnings engine is declining. When both fire against you together, that is what leads to such a brutal outcome. If only the multiple was compressing, you would still have had a plateau phase, not this massive drag. But you got hit on both.
And understand why that multiple is even compressing in the first place, because this is where most people stay blind.
A multiple is never random. It always follows the business state. When the business strengthens, when margins widen and the runway lengthens, the market re-rates it up. And when the business weakens, when growth collapses and the EPS engine stalls, that premium has no reason to exist anymore, so it reverts.
KPIT’s multiple is not falling on its own, it is falling because the business state behind it has weakened, and the market is simply repricing it to the new reality.
So this is the consequence of what happens when you overpay based on the past business data. And I know a lot of you have been trapped into these companies at 85, 88, 100 multiples. But this is how the compression happens.
And that leads to a lost decade, because the past on the screener has no meaning in investing. Because investing is all about the future: predicting the future state of the earning profile and the guidance. And whenever a thing is priced to perfection, the odds get brutally stacked against you over the long run.
But this also creates a window of opportunity in high-quality companies, because the whole sector gets de-rated. And you’ll even see that a company like Tata Elxsi, which was again ridiculously priced at 110 multiples in 2022, is compressing brutally. From 10,238 to 3,590 today, now down to 32 multiples.
And the odds are slowly and steadily shifting in your favour. But for that individual who bought the top, it’s a lost decade. 
Same de-rating, two different stories. One is a value trap, one is a window opening.
And don’t try to catch it here. There is another mental model I always use in investing, I call it 
The Foreshock.
 The first tremor is never the earthquake, it is only the warning before it.
The first guidance cut is the same, it is almost never the last one. Whenever a company signals a slowdown, one warning usually means many more are coming behind it, because managements don’t cut once and stabilise, they cut, then cut again, as the rot shows up in stages. And just like aftershocks, the bad news comes fast at first and then slowly decays.
So in the majority of cases you are far better off buying after 3-4 quarters of this, once the shaking has settled and the base is formed, not on the first crack. That is how you shift more odds into your favour.
So even if you buy a very high-quality company, you need to understand how the system operates. Don’t trust these analysts who were giving targets of 15,000 to 20,000 for these companies. Their incentives are not to make you money. And these fund managers were religiously buying it even after knowing that the valuation math of the decade was not in their favour.
But now the engines have started aligning again. And this will give you a decent opportunity to allocate over the long run. I still don’t like KPIT even here, the business quality doesn’t earn that premium from me.
But these are all living case studies on why not to overpay, especially on a large market cap. 
A 50 PE on a 10 to 20 thousand crore company is a very different animal from a 50 PE on a 1 lakh crore company,
 because on the larger base you have the odds against you to even generate that growth rate.
Think about it simply. If you are small, sitting on a 100 crore profit base with a 10,000 crore market cap, and you move the profit by just 30 crore, that is a 30% growth rate.
But when the size of the profit becomes 1,000 crore, and you still value it at 100 and give it a 1 lakh crore market cap based on past data, then even if the company grows profit by 100 crore, that is still just a 10% rate. And the market compresses that brutally.
So always adjust for market caps and growth rates. Anything which is close to or over one lakh crore, avoid paying higher multiples, because the law of large numbers and size will come into play. And you will be brutally cursed, and you will see a lost decade.
And even if you’re holding from lower levels, still you have to focus on what is the incremental growth from here, and whether you will buy the same stock at the fresh value or not. If the answer is no, you need to revisit the thesis again.
And always run the 
Two Engine Framework
 and the 
PE-and-growth framework.
 The PEG ratio in isolation itself will have no meaning, because you’re valuing it on the past data, and on the screener it was signalling a smaller PEG, but eventually you have to figure out the growth rates and the positioning of the future.
The screener tells you what a business was. Your job is to price what it becomes.
And this is the whole game, most people are still valuing tickers, not business states. They see KPIT at a “cheap” multiple now and think bargain, the same way they saw it at 100x and thought quality. Both are the same mistake, staring at the leaf instead of the roots. The multiple is just the leaf. The roots are the margin profile, the ROIC, the reinvestment runway, the concentration risk, the pricing power. When the roots deepen, the re-rating comes. When the roots rot, the de-rating comes. KPIT’s roots weakened, so the leaf fell. That is all this crash is.
So stop positioning on the ticker and the screener number. Position on the business state.
