Phase 1 — Screen
01

Read The Business, Not The Ticker

Business State vs. Ticker State

This is the lens every other stage sits inside, not a one-time checkbox. Before anything else, separate what the price is doing from what the business is doing. A stock can sit dead flat, crash, or spike for reasons that have nothing to do with the underlying company — and the entire method only works if this gap is checked first, before valuation, before a moat test, before anything.

Worked Example From The Tracker

Kovai Medical looked "boring" on a price chart for years while EPS quietly doubled underneath — the business state had moved enormously; the ticker just hadn't caught up. KPIT is the inverse warning: a screener showing "cheap after a crash" isn't automatically a bargain if the business state is also genuinely deteriorating underneath the falling ticker.

KOVAIKPITTECH
02

New Listing, IPO, or PE-Backed Story? — Ownership Screen

The Dealmaker ModelThe Shrinking Host ModelThe Pond TheoryThe Incentive ModelTrap Detection Framework ("Lollapalooza in Reverse")Percentage-of-Completion (POC) Accounting Trap

Only triggers for recent IPOs or a story built around a private-equity-owned company — but when it applies, it comes before valuation, because a structurally broken deal is broken at any price. Trace who owned this privately, at what price, and how fast they're selling to the public. Check whether the revenue model is pinned to a shrinking underlying asset. Check whether an unorganized industry is consolidating in a way that cuts this business out. Ask bluntly what the people in control are actually incentivized to do. Watch for revenue recognized before cash or customer approval exists.

Checkpoint
2+ ownership red flags stacking at once?
SKIP Ownership forensics failed — treat as a compounding red flag, not independent noise
CONTINUE Clean ownership chain, or only one minor flag
Worked Example From The Tracker

IGI failed on nearly every one of these at once (Blackstone's ~5x private-to-public flip in 20 months, a fee pinned to shrinking lab-grown-diamond values, certification trust migrating to brands). Airfloa failed the accounting and incentive tests specifically (POC-driven profit spike, negative free cash flow, IPO proceeds plugging a working-capital hole).

IGILAIRFLOA
Phase 2 — Analyze
03

Decompose The Return

The Two-Engine FrameworkLaw of Large Numbers (Size Ceiling)The Plateau FrameworkThe Depreciation Model / "Hidden Law of Great Compounders"

Once ownership clears, understand why the price is where it is. Split return into the earnings engine and the multiple engine — a stock can have real earnings growth and still be a bad investment if the multiple is simultaneously collapsing. Check whether the growth rate being paid for is even mathematically sustainable at the company's current size. If a good business looks "dead" on the chart for years, check whether earnings growth and multiple compression have simply been canceling out. And if reported profit growth looks like it's decelerating, check whether that's just front-loaded capex depreciation rather than a real slowdown.

Worked Example From The Tracker

HDFC Bank's EPS tripled while the stock barely moved — multiple compression ate the earnings engine. KPIT got hit by both engines reversing at once at an already-large market cap. Caplin Point's "decelerating" PAT growth was actually front-loaded capex depreciation, not a real slowdown.

HDFCBANKKPITTECHCAPLIPOINT
04

Stress-Test The Moat

The Caged Bird ModelThe Maginot Line Model

Before trusting any "durable moat" narrative, run two separate tests. First: was this loyalty or stickiness ever actually tested against a real alternative? Second: is the moat being attacked from the exact direction it was built to defend, or is a competitor bypassing it entirely from an angle it was never designed to face? A moat can be completely real and still become irrelevant.

Checkpoint
Does the moat survive the real attack?
RE-UNDERWRITE Bypassed, not beaten — the old moat doesn't face the new threat
CONTINUE Moat holds under genuine stress-testing
Worked Example From The Tracker

Sagility's 18-year client stickiness was measured entirely in a pre-AI world with no alternative (fails the first test), and its relationship-based moat was built to defend against other outsourcing firms, not against AI making the underlying labor obsolete (fails the second) — both models pointed the same direction independently.

SAGILITY
05

Check For Fresh Cracks

The Foreshock Model

If the stock just had a profit warning, a guidance cut, or a sudden crash, don't treat that single data point as either "priced in" or "the bottom." The base rate strongly favors more bad news following a first crack, not stabilization. This stage is about timing discipline on top of the analysis already done in Stages 3 and 4, not new analysis of its own.

Checkpoint
Is this the first tremor, or confirmed stable?
WAIT 3-4 quarters needed before treating this as a real opportunity
CONTINUE Stabilization already confirmed over several quarters
Worked Example From The Tracker

KPIT's 16% single-day crash was treated as a fresh warning, not a bottom, right after it happened. Trent is the sharper case — down 45% is read as the sixth or seventh slowdown in a pattern stretching back over a year, not "the crack" itself.

KPITTECHTRENT
06

Read Management & Capital Allocation

The Linguistic Mental ModelCapital Allocator Quality (ROCE vs. ROIIC)

Two checks on the people actually running the business. First, listen to how they literally talk on earnings calls — concrete numbers and firm timelines read differently than vague, hedged, storytelling language. Second, judge whether the incremental capital being deployed right now is actually earning good returns, and whether structures like a promoter pledge have a visible, tracked path down.

Worked Example From The Tracker

Kalyan Jewellers' promoter refusing to engage with a share-price question on a concall, paired with a pledge being paid down on a tracked schedule using operating cash flow, reframes what could look like two red flags into one honest operator being deliberately blunt about both.

KALYANKJIL
Phase 3 — Size & Time
07

Paradigm-Shift Overlay

The Bottleneck Strategy ("Road vs. Toll Booth")

Only activates when the entire sector is being re-architected by a technological shift — it doesn't apply to a stable, mature industry. When it does apply, map where the new, unavoidable chokepoint actually sits, and check whether a candidate owns that chokepoint or just operates near it. Some companies aren't a toll booth on someone else's road, they're building the road itself.

Worked Example From The Tracker

The entire cybersecurity basket was built by mapping five specific AI-era bottlenecks rather than picking the biggest legacy vendor. Cloudflare was reframed mid-thesis from "a toll booth" to "the road." Varonis was removed once its bottleneck was absorbed by larger platforms — proof this stage needs ongoing re-checking, not a one-time map.

08

Size The Conviction

The 100-Bagger Framework (Thomas Phelps)

Before deciding how much conviction a name deserves, check whether four specific forces stack together: does it cut a customer's cost, their time, and a structural constraint, and does adopting it create switching costs high enough that leaving becomes genuinely painful? This stage is about position sizing, not a buy/skip decision on its own.

Worked Example From The Tracker

Symbotic was sized with real conviction specifically because all four forces stacked — labor cost reduction, time reduction, and a switching cost that becomes close to unthinkable once a $500 million warehouse system is deployed.

SYM
09

Plan The Entry

Phoenix Forge & Dragon Flight Framework

Once a name clears Stages 1-8, the entry should be pre-planned, not decided emotionally. Is a falling price a "Forge" (business strengthening while the ticker sleeps) or a "Pyre" (business deteriorating — a trap dressed as a discount)? Once confirmed as a Forge, pre-commit to price zones for adding on the way down, and separate tiers for adding on the way to new highs.

Worked Example From The Tracker

Caplin Point's accumulation zones (roughly ₹1,350-2,050 on the way down) and later "Dragon" tiers (₹2,020-2,971 on the way up) were both mapped out ahead of time, because Stages 1-6 had already confirmed a strengthening business.

CAPLIPOINT
Phase 4 — Maintain
10

Add Over Time, Not On Price

The Snowball Method

For positions already held, the rule for adding more is explicitly not about price — it's about whether the business keeps proving new, independent reasons to own it. If every variable that mattered at the original purchase is still getting stronger, that's the signal to add, regardless of price.

Worked Example From The Tracker

Symbotic was added to at $14 and again at $24 specifically because the customer list, backlog value, and moat evidence had each independently strengthened — not because of where the price happened to be sitting.

11

Loop — Re-Run Stage 1 Continuously

This isn't a separate model, it's the reminder that the whole framework is a loop, not a one-time checklist. The business state keeps moving whether or not anyone is watching it — a name that cleared every stage six months ago needs Stage 1 run again today.

Worked Example From The Tracker

This is the same discipline that let the framework catch Varonis's thesis breaking mid-post (Stage 7 re-checked) and Kovai's plateau resolving upward (Stage 1 and 3 re-checked) — the loop, not any single stage, is what the method actually is.
